I.
Public health care
1.
What is the structure of health care in Poland?
In Poland, there is public health care, available to every insured
person, financed from public funds, and paid health care. In our
publication we will look at public health care.
Some facilities provide both paid and free health services,
remember to make sure when registering for an appointment that
it is the National Health Fund (NFZ)-contracted service - free of
charge.
2.
Am I entitled to free medical care?
Yes. Every person working in Poland is subject to compulsory
health insurance. Health insurance aims to protect people's health.
As part of the insurance, everyone is entitled to free treatment,
diagnostics, nursing care, rehabilitation, hospital stay, spa and
preventive health care. If you are insured in Poland, you have the
same access to medical care as insured Polish citizens.
3.
How can I get insurance?
If you are employed on the basis of a contract of employment or a
contract of mandate, your employer will report you to the Social
Insurance Institution (ZUS) for health insurance, and the insurance
premiums will be paid from your salary. If you are self-employed,
you must register for insurance with the Social Insurance
Institution yourself. Contributions from ZUS are transferred to the
7

--- strona 8 ---
National Health Fund (NFZ). It is a state organisational unit for the
financing of health services.
4.
Can I insure my wife and children?
Register all non-working family members living with you - spouse,
child, parents or grandparents - for health insurance. The
registration of family members is free and unlimited (you can
register as many family members as you wish).
5.
How do I choose my doctor and clinic?
You can choose any Primary Health Care (POZ) facility in your
province. It is most convenient if the facility is located close to
your home. You will need to go to a clinic of your choice (known
as a POZ facility) and fill in a declaration of choice for a doctor,
nurse and, for women, a midwife.
Forms for declarations of choice can be found at the end of this
publication. Declarations can also be made through your online
patient account (IKP). You can change your POZ doctor twice a
year for free.
You can find a list of POZ clinics on the NFZ website:
https://gsl.nfz.gov.pl/GSL/GSL/.

II.
Primary Health Care (POZ)
1.
Who is a POZ doctor?
The POZ care doctor treats adults (specialist in internal medicine
or family medicine) and children (specialist in paediatrics or child
diseases or specialist in family medicine).
2.
What does a POZ doctor do?
The POZ doctor manages the entire treatment process. His/her
remit includes:
•
Treating patients and giving advice.
•
Establish and monitor pharmacological treatment.
•
Issuing prescriptions and orders for medical supplies.
•
Issuing of sick notes.
•
Referral for preventive vaccinations and periodic check-ups.
•
Sending for tests, e.g. laboratory diagnosis, ECG, X-ray,
ultrasound.
•
Interpretation of test results.
•
Referral to spa and rehabilitation treatment.
•
Referral to specialist doctors and hospital.
•
Referral to nursing long-term home care.
•
Health adjudication.
•
Issuing an order for the services of a POZ nurse.

3.
How do I make an appointment with a POZ
doctor?
Most often the appointment with the doctor is set by telephone, in
special cases the appointment is made on the day of registration,
in other cases on average within 3-5 days.
4.
What documents do I need to take with me to the
POZ appointment?
Always take with you an identity document with a photo and
PESEL number, and in the case of a child, a medical record book.
For your first appointment, take all your medical records from your
treatment to date, and a list of the drugs you are taking. If it is
your next appointment, take the results of any tests, medical
consultations that your POZ has not yet seen.
You can find a short guide on how to prepare for a visit to POZ on
this website:
https://www.nfz-szczecin.pl/jqt1c_zanim_pojdziesz_do_
lekarza_rodzinnego.htm
5.
Does the consultation of the POZ doctor always
take place at the clinic?
When the patient's state of health prevents a visit to the clinic, the
visit may be carried out at the place of residence by prior
arrangement with the doctor. This is known as a home visit. If a
problem does not require examination by a doctor, you can get
10

--- strona 11 ---
help over the phone. This is known as an online doctor
consultation.
6.
Can I also go to the POZ clinic with my child?
Yes. Your POZ clinic should have a paediatrician or family doctor
who can diagnose and treat paediatric conditions and carry out
preventive vaccination and well-child cares. Remember that in
order for your child to receive services at a POZ clinic, you must
make a declaration of choice of doctor.
7.
Who is a POZ nurse?
The primary health care (POZ) nurse is a community nurse. You
can make a declaration of choice by going to a POZ or via your
online patient account.
The nurse cares for patients both in the clinic and at home. The
tasks of the community nurse include:
•
Drug administration.
•
Performing injections.
•
Removal of sutures.
•
Performing inhalations.
•
Carrying out nursing and therapeutic procedures.
8.
Who is a POZ midwife?
The POZ midwife provides comprehensive nursing, obstetric and
gynaecological care. The POZ midwife cares for women from 21
weeks of pregnancy and newborns. Up to 2 calendar months of
11

--- strona 12 ---
the child's life, the POZ midwife is obliged to make 4 - 6 patronage
visits. You can meet the midwife at her office at the clinic or at
your home, depending on the situation.
The tasks of the midwife include:
•
Care for pregnant women.
•
Recognising health problems.
•
Planning and implementation of preventive services.
•
Education of women with high-risk pregnancies.
•
Care of newborns.
•
Examination of children including assessment of general
condition, adaptation of the child to the environment.
•
Helping to create a bond between mother and child and to
initiate breastfeeding.
•
Promotion of parents' health-seeking behaviour.
9.
What hours does POZ provide care?
You can go to the POZ clinic every day from Monday to Friday
from 8:00 am to 6:00 pm. When your POZ clinic is closed, you can
go to a night and holiday healthcare facility.
III.
Online patient account
1.
What is an online patient account (IKP)?
It is a tool that organises scattered medical information. Anyone
with a PESEL has IKP, but only adults can use this account.

To use IKP you need to log on to the website:
https://pacjent.gov.pl/. For this you need an electronic
signature, which can be a trusted profile, a temporary trusted
profile or an e-ID card.
IKP provides patients with information about their past, current or
planned treatment and allows them to deal with a range of issues
without having to visit a clinic or advice clinic. It gives online
access to your full medical history, the visits you have made to the
clinic or doctor, the care you have received, and how much NFZ
has paid for these services. It gives access to e-prescriptions,
information on issued referrals and their status, e-sick notes and
medical certificates issued in connection with illness and maternity.
IKP also provides access to information on minor children (if you
have insured them) and family members who have provided us
with access to their health information. There is also a free app for
your phone – "mojeikp".

IV.
Prescription
1.
What is a prescription?
A prescription is a document issued by a doctor on the basis of
which a pharmacist can sell you a particular drug.
14

--- strona 15 ---
2.
What drugs are prescribed?
A prescription is usually needed to purchase antibiotics and
medicines ordered by medical specialists that would be dangerous
to use on their own. These include, for example, sleeping pills,
sedatives and anti-epileptic drugs.
3.
Which drugs can I buy without a prescription?
Basic painkillers and antipyretics, some medicines used to treat the
common cold, some ointments can be purchased without a
prescription.
4.
How do I get a prescription?
The prescription is issued by a doctor, less often by a nurse, in a
face-to-face visit or during online doctor consultation after taking a
history and planning the treatment. The prescription can be a 4-
digit PIN number or a printout with a barcode or QR code. PIN will
be given by your doctor, but you also have it in your IKP, and if
you select this option in your IKP, you will receive it by SMS or e-
mail.
5.
I have a prescription and what happens next?
You apply the prescription at the pharmacy by entering your
PESEL number and 4-digit PIN number or by showing the barcode
or QR code of the prescription.
15

--- strona 16 ---
6.
How do I buy drugs at a discount?
Some medicines can be bought more cheaply through
reimbursement. When issuing the prescription, the doctor
determines the amount of the discount depending on the diagnosis
he has made (chronic diseases, certain infections). For patients
aged 75 and over, some drugs are free of charge.
7.
Can my e-prescription become out of date?
Yes. The validity of the prescription may depend on the type of a
drug. Most prescriptions are valid for 30 days.
Exceptions:
•
a prescription for an oral or injectable antibiotic is valid for 7
days (in the case of antibiotics in ointments or drops, the
prescription is normally valid for 30 days)
•
a prescription issued for 365 days - medicines can be bought
at one time or in several packs, but at the same pharmacy.
If you buy your first pack later than 30 days, your
pharmacist will reduce the number of packs. You can buy
medicines for 180 days of therapy at a time.
•
The prescription for immunological preparations individually
prepared (e.g. vaccines for desensitisation) and imported
from abroad is valid for 120 days. This does not apply to
mass-produced vaccines - e.g. against influenza - such a
prescription is only valid for 30 days.

V.
Sick note
1.
What is electronic sick note (eZLA)?
A doctor may issue a certificate justifying an employee's absence
due to illness - so-called eZLA (commonly called L4 from the name
of the form). The employer downloads the sick note in electronic
form from his profile on the ZUS Electronic Services Platform (PUE
ZUS). In rare cases, the employer does not use PUE ZUS, in which
case the doctor will print out the sick note which you must provide
to your employer within 7 days.
2.
How long can I be on a sick note?
You are entitled to a maximum of 182 days of sick leave, the
exception being sick leave during pregnancy or for tuberculosis - a
maximum of 270 days.
3.
What if I am no longer entitled to sick note but
am still ill?
If you have exhausted the maximum number of days of sick leave
and your entitlement to sick pay has run out, you can apply to the
Social Insurance Office for rehabilitation benefit for 12 months.
4.
Am I entitled to leave from work if a child or other
family member is ill?
Yes. A doctor may issue a certificate justifying an employee's
absence due to his or her illness or the illness of persons under his
17

--- strona 18 ---
or her care. An eZLA issued to a parent who cannot continue
working because of the child's illness is a carer's benefit.
Annually can be used:
•
60 days of care for a sick child up to 14 years
•
14 days of care for a sick child aged 14-18 or other family
member.
•
30 days for illness of disabled children aged 14 to 18.
5.
Is the salary during leave the same as during
work?
Sickness and carer's benefit is 80% of salary.
It can be 100% of salary if:
•
it is the sickness benefit during pregnancy
•
the illness is caused by an accident at work or on the way to
or from work
•
absence from work is due to participation in medical
procedures foreseen for donors of cells, tissues and organs
6.
Will my employer find out what I am ill with?
The eZLA form received by the employer does not contain a
diagnosis of the illness. Sometimes the sick note will include one of
the letter codes that specify the circumstances of the illness
affecting the benefit.
A code - the inability to work has occurred after an interval of not
more than 60 days, following the same disease which was the
cause of the inability to work before the interval
18

--- strona 19 ---
B code - inability to work during pregnancy.
C code - inability to work due to alcohol abuse. An employee who
has received sick note with the C code is not entitled to wages or
benefits for the first 5 days from the date the sick note began.
D code - inability to work due to tuberculosis.
E code - inability to work due to an infectious disease that has an
incubation period of more than 14 days or another disease whose
symptoms manifest themselves after more than 14 days from the
beginning of the disease.
On your written request, the doctor may choose not to enter codes
B and D.
7.
Can I get a back-dated or forward-dated sick
note?
Usually the doctor will issue a sick note with the date of the
appointment. A sick note can be written up to a maximum of 3
days back from the medical examination or online doctor
consultation. An exception is a sick note from a psychiatrist, which
can be written out at an even earlier date. The sick note can also
be written forward with the date of the next day or, if the
following days are public holidays, with the date on which you
should be at work.
19

--- strona 20 ---
8.
Do I need to do any surveys before returning to
work?
If you have been ill for more than 30 days you need a certificate
from your POZ doctor stating that you are able to return to work.
After receiving this certificate, your employer must refer you to an
occupational doctor for a check-up to determine whether you can
work in the same position. The cost of the tests is covered by the
employer.
VI. Primary health care – children.
1.
What kind of care can my child expect at POZ?
Usually the doctor's work is divided into consultations of sick and
healthy children. As part of the sick children's consultation,
children with symptoms of infection are welcomed. During the
reception hours for healthy children there are vaccinations, well-
child cares and sometimes diagnostics for diseases that are not
caused by an acute infection-e.g. abdominal pain, eating
disorders, abnormal growth.
2.
What is a well-child care?
It should be carried out in children aged 1-4 weeks, 2-6 months, 9
months, 12 months (on dates corresponding to preventive
vaccinations), 2 years, 4 years, 5 years, one-year compulsory pre-
school preparation, 3rd grade of primary school, 1st grade of lower
20

--- strona 21 ---
secondary school,1st grade of upper secondary school, the last
year of upper secondary school until the age of 19
Children who do not yet go to school have their well-child care
done entirely in POZ. School-age children start their well-child care
at the school nurse's office (weighing, measuring, blood pressure
check, eye examination, posture check). There, after an initial
examination, they receive a card with which they must go to the
POZ clinic.
The doctor checks that height, weight pressure, posture, visual
acuity are normal. He/she performs a general medical
examination, evaluates the implementation of the preventive
vaccinations. If necessary, he/she issues referrals to other
specialists or laboratory tests.
3.
How do I vaccinate my child?
Children staying in Poland for more than 3 months should continue
to be vaccinated to match the vaccination calendar. To do this,
you need to make an appointment with a paediatrician or family
doctor at chosen POZ (where you have made your declarations).
At the time of registration it should be indicated that this is a
vaccination. For vaccination, it is necessary to prepare documents
in which the previously administered vaccines are recorded (e.g.
the child's medical record book).
21

--- strona 22 ---
4.
What is the vaccination calendar?
The vaccination calendar is a list of compulsory vaccinations with
an indication of the age at which they are due. It is updated
annually by the Chief Sanitary Inspector.
5.
How is my child vaccinated?
The doctor examines the child to rule out any contraindications to
vaccination. Once your child is eligible for vaccination you will be
asked to sign a consent form. The vaccination is carried out by a
nurse. After vaccination, observation of the patient is carried out
for a minimum of 15 minutes. If there are no worrying symptoms
during this time, you can leave the clinic.
6.
Where to get the vaccine from?
Compulsory vaccinations are free and available at the POZ. Each
year, the Ministry of Health determines which vaccines will be used
to implement the vaccination calendar.
If you want to choose a vaccine other than the one chosen by the
Ministry you must buy it. You can buy the vaccine at some clinics
(you have to ask about it when you register for your appointment)
or ask your doctor in advance to write a prescription and buy it at
a pharmacy. Vaccines purchased outside the clinic must be stored
in your refrigerator on the middle shelf, away from the refrigerator
wall, never in the refrigerator door. You need to bring it to the
clinic in a heat insulated bag.

VII. Organisation of preventive vaccination
1.
Where can I get vaccinated?
The vaccination can be carried out at vaccination clinics, most of
which are located in POZ.
23

--- strona 24 ---
2.
Where can my child be vaccinated?
Vaccination takes place at vaccination centres. During the
COVID19 pandemic, a number of population-based vaccination
centres were established but the majority of vaccinations,
including for children, take place in POZ.
3.
What vaccinations should I complete?
Vaccinations are scheduled based on the vaccination calendar,
which is updated annually. For children staying in a country for
more than 3 months, vaccinations should be completed according
to the vaccination calendar of the country concerned. Some
vaccinations should be repeated even in adult patients (e.g.
against diphtheria, tetanus and whooping cough, against
influenza), and their schedule can be agreed with the POZ doctor.
VIII. Night and holiday health care
1.
What is night and holiday health care?
When a POZ clinic is closed, the role of POZ is taken over by night
and holiday health care clinics (so-called 24-hour care). If you feel
unwell and are concerned that your health will deteriorate by the
next day but is not life-threatening, go to your nearest night and
holiday health care clinic. If you feel unwell but your condition is
stable, wait until the next day and see your POZ doctor.
24

--- strona 25 ---
2.
What are the hours of night and holiday health
care?
From 6pm to 8am and around the clock on public holidays.
3.
Where can I find the address of night and holiday
health care facilities?
You can find the nearest night and holiday health care clinic on the
website of the National Health Fund:
https://www.nfz.gov.pl/dla-pacjenta/na-ratunek/.
4.
What problems should I bring to the night and
holiday healthcare facility?
You can take advantage of night and holiday health care if you are
suddenly ill and cannot wait until your POZ clinic is open due to
the severity of your symptoms. This does not apply if your life is in
danger, in which case you go to SOR.
IX. Casualty department (SOR)
1.
What is SOR?
SOR is part of the National Emergency Medical Service. It is
designed for patients with life-threatening conditions. In SOR, an
initial diagnosis is made and vital functions are stabilised.
25

--- strona 26 ---
2.
How long will I wait for help in SOR?
A medical segregation procedure (triage) is carried out on each
patient. The time during which aid is granted depends on the
category granted:
•
red – immediate contact with a doctor
•
orange – waiting time for first contact with a doctor up to 10
minutes
•
yellow – waiting time for first contact with a doctor up to 60
minutes
•
green – waiting time for first contact with a doctor up to 120
minutes
•
blue – waiting time for first contact with a doctor up to 240
minutes
In practice, SOR has the highest number of patients in the green
and blue groups who should not really be presenting to SOR.
Therefore, waiting times can extend to several hours.
3.
What problems can I bring to SOR?
People with a sudden health emergency should come to SOR.
These include injuries (e.g. wounds, joint sprains, contusions
suspected to be fractures), burns, frostbite, allergies.
You will also be brought to SOR by the emergency medical service
from the scene of an accident or from home if your condition
suddenly deteriorates (e.g. persistent shortness of breath, chest
pain, loss of consciousness).
26

--- strona 27 ---
4.
What problems should not be brought to SOR?
Do not present to SOR for:
•
obtaining specialist advice
•
writing a prescription
•
obtaining a sick note
•
changing dressings
•
injections
•
removing stitches from the wound
•
removing plaster dressings
•
catheter exchanges
•
performance of laboratory tests
•
in case of symptoms of infection that are not life-threatening
(do not come in because of a sore throat, runny nose,
cough)
5.
What documents should I have when coming to
SOR?
You need your PESEL number and identity card or passport.
X.
Emergency medical service
1.
What is an emergency medical service?
The emergency medical service provides assistance in
emergencies. For life-threatening emergencies, call the emergency
number 999 or 112. The dispatcher will decide on the need for a
medical emergency team based on the history taken.
27

--- strona 28 ---
2.
When to call an emergency medical service?
You call the emergency medical service if you need help with life-
threatening emergencies such as chest pain, paresis, speech
impairment, impaired consciousness, traffic accidents.
3.
How to call an emergency medical service?
You call an emergency medical service by dialling 112 or 999.
4.
Will the emergency medical service take me to a
hospital?
The paramedic or doctor will decide whether you should be taken
to a hospital. The medical emergency team will decide which SOR
you go to – this is usually SOR closest to the scene of the incident,
where adequate medical attention can be given.
XI. Outpatient specialist services (AOS)
1.
When should I make an appointment for a
specialist clinic?
You go to a specialist clinic if a particular health problem cannot
be fully diagnosed and treated in the POZ clinic. In most cases you
will need a referral from your POZ doctor to make an appointment.
In practice, he/she decides at what point you should go to the
specialist clinic.
Specialist clinics with a contract with the NFZ do not accept
referrals issued by doctors during private consultations.
28

--- strona 29 ---
2.
How should I make an appointment for a
specialist clinic?
For most outpatient clinics, you will need a referral from your
health insurance doctor (a doctor from the POZ or specialist clinic).
You receive a 4-digit code, so-called PIN.
It can be printed by your doctor, or you will receive it by SMS or
email (if you set this option in IKP).
You can make an appointment by phone or in person. When
making an appointment at the clinic, you give this number and
your PESEL number.
3.
Do I need a referral to every specialist clinic?
No. There are exceptions. You do not need a referral to a
psychiatrist, gynaecologist-obstetrician, oncologist, venereologist,
dentist.
4.
How long is the wait for specialist advice?
The wait for an appointment varies, sometimes many months. If
the proposed date is very far away you can ask for a date at
another clinic. If you need urgent treatment because of your state
of health, the doctor will add this information on the referral (the
"pilne" annotation).
You will find information on dates of treatment at specialist clinics,
hospitals and surgery on the website:
https://terminyleczenia.nfz.gov.pl/

XII. Stay in hospital wards
1.
Who can make a referral to a hospital?
You can be referred to the hospital by your health insurance
doctor or by a doctor from a private practice.
2.
Do I have to agree to the proposed treatment?
You have the right not to consent to hospitalisation. In this case,
the doctor will ask you for a written statement of refusal to
consent to the proposed treatment.
3.
What rights does the patient have?

The right to information about one's own health. A person of
the patient's choice may obtain information about the
patient's health. The authorisation may be in writing or oral
form.
•
The right to refuse treatment
•
The right to medical confidentiality
•
The right to a supplementary medical opinion
•
The right to own medical records. It may also be made
available to a statutory representative or a person authorised
by the patient.
•
The right to be accompanied by a close relative
•
The right to pastoral care or the right of direct contact with a
priest of the religion professed. You should let the medical
staff know that you would like to see a priest of your faith. It

•
The right to treatment of pain
•
The right to respect for intimacy and personal dignity
•
The right to complain

Is the hospital obliged to provide an interpreter
for a foreign language patient?
The hospital has no legal obligation to provide the patient with an
interpreter. Hospitals often create a list of staff members fluent in
foreign languages on their own initiative. If you have any problems
communicating, please contact the head office or regional branch

XIII. Pregnancy and childbirth
1.
Who can carry a pregnancy?
By law, a pregnancy can be carried out by a doctor or midwife. In
practice, this is usually a doctor.
2.
I am pregnant, when should I see a doctor and do
I need a referral?
It is best to see an obstetrician-gynaecologist of your choice by
the 10th week of the pregnancy. A visit to the gynaecologist does
not require a referral.
31

--- strona 32 ---
3.
In what does medical care during pregnancy
consist?
The doctor carrying out the pregnancy will order tests according to
Polish standards. You can also choose to receive coordinated care
for pregnant women (KOC) at a maternity hospital at any stage of
your pregnancy. The woman then receives full-profile care during
pregnancy, childbirth and for six weeks after the birth of the child.
The safety of mum and baby will be supervised by a team
consisting of a gynaecologist, midwife and hospital doctors.
4.
Am I entitled to an ultrasound scan of my
pregnancy?
An ultrasonography is performed at the first visit to confirm the
pregnancy. Many doctors perform an ultrasonography at every
visit. Around the 12th and 20th weeks of pregnancy, a thorough
ultrasonography, known as a genetic ultrasonography, is
performed to assess the health of the baby.
5.
What are prenatal tests?
Prenatal tests are examinations of the foetus during intrauterine
development. The purpose of the tests is to detect congenital
diseases or genetic defects. Tests are performed in pregnant
women who meet one of the conditions:
•
have reached the age of 35
•
there was a genetic defect in a previous pregnancy
32

--- strona 33 ---
•
the pregnant woman or the father of the child has a known
genetic defect
•
the doctor has identified a significantly increased risk of
having a child affected by a genetic or multifactorial
condition
•
the result of an ultrasonography or biochemical test
performed during pregnancy is abnormal
6.
Can I work while the pregnant?
If you feel well you can work during the pregnancy, but you
cannot take an overtime. You don't have to work at night (night
work is only possible with your permission). If you are worried
about your child or your health requires it, you are on sick leave.
Any sick leave during pregnancy entitles you to sick pay at 100%
of the base.
7.
I am pregnant, can my employer dismiss me for
this reason?
An employer may not terminate or dissolve an employment
contract during pregnancy and maternity leave of an employee.
8.
Do pregnant women have special rights?
Yes. Pregnant women are entitled to health care services and
pharmaceutical services provided in pharmacies out of turn. "Out
of turn" provision in specialist clinics means admission on the day
33

--- strona 34 ---
of registration or at another time, no later than within 7 working
days of registration.
Some drugs for pregnant women are free or reimbursed if the
doctor mentions this on the prescription (letter B). Free drugs
include: insulin, lutein, levothyroxine, metoprolol.
9.
Who is a community midwife?
If you are pregnant you should choose a POZ community midwife.
You will need to give the details of the POZ midwife and doctor,
you choose for your baby at the hospital after the baby is born.
The POZ midwife cares for women from 21 weeks of pregnancy
and newborns up to 2 calendar months of the child's life. The POZ
midwife is required to make 4-6 patronage visits to the child's
home. The first visit takes place no later than 48 hours after the
mother and child leave the hospital.
The parents should choose a midwife before the baby is born and
notify her when the newborn is discharged home.
10. Where in Poland do women give birth?
Women in Poland give birth in hospitals, in maternity wards. There
are no birthing rooms in Poland. Home births by choice are rare.
11.
What can I do if I have problems with
breastfeeding when I return home?
Ask your community midwife for help. There are also lactation
clinics at maternity wards, which you can use to help you.

--- strona 35 ---
12. What formalities should I do after the birth?
The birth of a child must be reported within 14 days of the birth to
the register office (USC). USC will issue the birth certificate,
register the child and assign a PESEL number. Once you have
received PESEL number, you must register your child for insurance
at the workplace of one of the parents or at ZUS. You then need
to choose a POZ doctor (paediatrician or family doctor) for your
child and arrange a patronage visit up to 4 weeks after the birth of
your child (usually 2 weeks after birth).
13. How long does the maternity leave last?
The length of maternity leave depends on the number of children
born:
•
20 weeks if one child is born in a single birth,
•
31 weeks if two children are born in a single birth,
•
33 weeks if three children are born in a single birth,
An employee may take up to six weeks of maternity leave before
the expected date of childbirth.
If the employee has not taken maternity leave before childbirth,
the maternity leave shall start on the day of childbirth.
14. Do I have to take all my maternity leave?
After childbirth, the mother must compulsorily take 14 weeks of
leave. She may give up the remaining six weeks of leave and
return to work, provided that the remaining maternity leave is

--- strona 36 ---
taken by the other carer bringing up the child, who has ceased
paid activity in order to provide care.
15. What is the parental leave?
The parental leave serves the purpose of continuing to care for a
young child. It is a condition for an employee to take parental
leave, irrespective of which parent of the child will be using it, that
maternity leave is taken in advance and that the application is
submitted to the employer within 21 days of the birth of the child.
The parental leave is up to 32 weeks for the birth of one child and
up to 34 weeks for the simultaneous birth of more than one child.
The parental leave may be taken:
•
entirely by one of the employees-parents,
•
by both employees-parents at the same time, but then the
periods of leave taken by the employee-mother and the
employee-father are added together and may not exceed 32
weeks for one child and 34 weeks for more than one child
born at the same time
•
alternating between the two parents, who will share the 32
weeks.
16. Will my pay change during my maternity and
parental leave?
You are entitled to be paid 80% of your salary for the whole year
or 100% for 20 weeks, and 60% on your parental leave.

--- strona 37 ---
17. What is the childcare leave?
The childcare leave is granted for the personal care of a child to an
employee who has been employed for at least 6 months. Previous
periods of employment are also included in this period of
employment. The childcare leave can be taken by both mother and
father if both are employees.
This leave lasts for a maximum of 36 months. It can be divided
into up to 5 parts. You can use it until the end of the calendar year
in which your child turns 6. In the case of the childcare leave to
care for a disabled child, you can take it until the child turn 18.
XIV. Dental care

Do I need a referral to go to the dentist?
You do not need a referral to visit a dentist (dental practitioner).
2.
Why do Poles choose private dental care?
Poles are opting for private dental care due to the significantly
reduced reimbursable benefits. White fillings are only reimbursed
in teeth 1 to 3, in the case of adults these are chemically-cured
fillings, in the case of children they are light-cured. Reimbursed
root canal treatment is only available for teeth 1 to 3. Orthodontic
treatment with removable braces only applies until the age of 12.
Significant limitations also apply to prosthetics.

What to do in case of a sudden toothache?
In the case of a sudden toothache, you should be admitted to a
clinic with a NFZ contract on the same day. If you have a
toothache when clinics are closed (at night or on a public holiday)
go to an emergency dental service.

XV. Medical rehabilitation
1.
With which medical conditions can I apply for
rehabilitation?
Medical rehabilitation is a method of treatment for many diseases
of the musculoskeletal system, the nervous system, the circulatory
system and the respiratory system. It is used after surgical
operations, including oncological ones.
2.
What does rehabilitation treatment look like?
A referral from your POZ doctor is required for rehabilitation.
Rehabilitation can take place:
•
ambulatory – for patients requiring rehabilitation or
physiotherapy who move independently
•
at home – for patients requiring rehabilitation or
physiotherapy who not move independently

--- strona 39 ---
•
in a centre or day unit – for patients whose health condition
does not allow rehabilitation in an ambulatory setting, but
who do not require round-the-clock medical supervision
•
stationary – for patients who require complex rehabilitation
services and round-the-clock medical and nursing
supervision due to continuing treatment.
For more information, visit:
https://www.gov.pl/web/zdrowie/rehabilitacja-
lecznicza
3.
What is rehabilitation benefit?
The rehabilitation benefit is a kind of cash benefit for persons who
have used up all their sick leave due to illness and are still unable
to work, and further medical treatment or rehabilitation is likely to
restore their ability to work within a period of up to 12 months. It
is paid by ZUS or by the employer.
XVI. Spa treatment
1.
What is spa treatment?
Spa treatment is a continuation of inpatient or outpatient
treatment. If you require certain treatments at spas in order to
recover from an illness or accident, or because of an occupational
disease, you may benefit from this type of treatment. The spa
uses mineral waters, mud, climatotherapeutic stimuli,
hydrotherapy, physical therapy, kinesitherapy.
39

--- strona 40 ---
Only patients who are fit enough to travel to the spa and who are
independent may be referred for spa treatment.
2.
Is a referral required for spa treatment?
A referral for spa treatment is issued by your health insurance
doctor, taking into account your current state of health.
3.
I have received a referral for spa treatment and
what next?
The referral is sent by the doctor or by you to the regional branch
of NFZ. It should be sent within 30 days of the date of issue,
otherwise it is no longer valid. It is then checked by a doctor
employed by the Fund, who assesses its advisability and indicate
the type and location of spa treatment.
If the specialist determines that there are contraindications or no
indications for treatment, he/she does not confirm the referral. It
will be returned to the doctor who issued it and you will receive
written information. The decision is final and cannot be appealed.
If a date of treatment is agreed, the regional branch of NFZ will
provide you with a confirmed referral up to 14 days before the
date of treatment.
4.
How long does spa treatment last?
For an adult, a stay in a spa hospital lasts 21 days. The child's stay
lasts 27 days. Such a stay is free of charge.

--- strona 41 ---
The stay in the spa sanatorium lasts 21 days. The stay of a child is
free of charge, while the stay of an adult is partially charged.
Stay in a spa hospital for spa rehabilitation lasts 28 days and is
free of charge.
Stay in a spa sanatorium for spa rehabilitation lasts 28 days and is
partially charged.
Ambulatory spa treatment of adults and children lasts from 6 to 18
days.
For more information, visit: https://www.nfz.gov.pl/dla-

What should I do if I suspect I have an addiction?

addicted to alcohol or psychotropic substances you do not need a

Drug treatment for addiction is voluntary, except where there is a
court obligation to treat. Treatment can be inpatient in a hospital
or ambulatory.

1.
What should I do if I suspect I have cancer?

What is a karta DiLO?
With a karta DiLO card, a patient with a suspected cancer enters
